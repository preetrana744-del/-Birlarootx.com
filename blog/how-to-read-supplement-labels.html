Label literacy

How to Read a Supplement Label Before You Buy

A practical guide to checking serving sizes, ingredient amounts, directions, cautions and batch details before choosing a supplement.

By Birla RootX · 15 October 2026 · 3 min read

In this guide
  1. Start with the exact product
  2. Separate a serving from the whole pack
  3. Read the ingredient list and cautions together
  4. Check the practical pack details
  5. Keep your questions specific

Start with the exact product

Read the full product name, format and pack quantity before comparing two bottles. A capsule count tells you how many capsules are in the pack; it does not tell you the amount of every ingredient. Keep the product page and the current physical label together when checking details. Packaging can change, so a saved image from an old listing may not describe the pack you receive.

Separate a serving from the whole pack

Locate the serving size, then check whether quantities are stated per serving, per capsule or per 100 g. These are different bases for comparison. As an arithmetic example only, a hypothetical 60-capsule pack with a stated serving of two capsules contains 30 such servings. That example is not a direction for any Birla RootX product. Follow the directions and limits on the exact label rather than borrowing a serving size from another product.

Read the ingredient list and cautions together

Review the full list, including ingredients beyond the main headline ingredient. Note any amounts, allergens, age restrictions and other cautions printed on the pack. Do not assume two products with similar names contain the same formula. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements consumer guide explains why ingredient information matters and why supplements can interact with medicines. Ask a healthcare professional about personal suitability; a product comparison cannot answer that question.

Check the practical pack details

Find the batch or lot identifier, relevant date marking, storage instructions and manufacturer or marketer contact details. Keep the original packaging while using the product so these details remain available if you have a question. If an image is too small to read, request a clearer label rather than guessing. For products sold in India, consult FSSAI’s health supplement information for the relevant regulatory framework; this article does not certify a product’s compliance.

Keep your questions specific

Before ordering, write down what is missing: the serving basis, a hard-to-read caution or a batch-specific detail. Ask support for product information and your clinician for medical advice. Birla RootX product pages show transcribed label details, while the physical pack is the reference for the batch you receive. Explore the Performance Series and open an individual product to compare its label information. A name or promotional phrase should never replace reading those details.

General education, not personalised medical advice. This article has not been medically reviewed and does not establish the safety or effectiveness of any Birla RootX product. Read the current physical label and ask a qualified healthcare professional about personal suitability.
